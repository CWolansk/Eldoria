## Overview
**Type**: Social (can escalate to Combat)
**Location**: Any road during travel — use when it feels right
**Party Level**: 5
**Difficulty**: Hard (social stakes, not XP)
**Floating**: Yes — drop in during any overland travel

A Rakshasa named **Mahavan the Collector** sits cross-legged in the middle of the road, disguised as a weathered old man in patched robes, playing a golden flute. The music is hauntingly beautiful — audible from 300 ft. He challenges [[Private/1. The Party/Players/Liz|Liz]] to a musical duel. Her soul against his flute.

Mahavan collects mortal artistry. He heard Liz play at some point (he's been following the party in disguise for days) and wants to know if she's *truly* gifted or just another competent musician. He's not malicious — he's **vain, theatrical, and bored**. This is sport.

Think "Devil and Daniel Webster" — a fiend who treats soul-wagers as civilized entertainment.

## Hook

> The road ahead bends around a copse of silver birch, and that's where the music starts. Not birdsong — something deliberate, something *played*. Around the bend, an old man sits cross-legged in the middle of the packed earth, barefoot, eyes closed. His robes are patched and sun-bleached. His fingers move across a golden flute that catches the light like a small sun. The melody is unlike anything you've heard — notes that seem to linger in the air a half-beat longer than they should, as if the wind itself is reluctant to carry them away.
>
> He opens one eye as you approach. Smiles. Doesn't stop playing.

When he finally stops, he speaks to **Liz specifically**. He's warm, charming, grandfatherly. He compliments her bardic talent (he's heard her play — he doesn't explain how). Then he makes his offer.

> "I'm an old man with a beautiful instrument and no legacy to leave it to. You — yes, you with the elf-ears and the musician's hands — you might be worthy. Play me a tune. If yours is better than mine, the flute is yours. If mine is better..." He turns the flute over in his fingers. "Well. We'll discuss terms."

If pressed on terms: he wants her **name signed in his ledger** — a small leather book he produces from his robes. He frames it as "a record of our wager, nothing more." It is, in fact, a soul contract written in Infernal. DC 18 Arcana or DC 16 Religion to recognize the binding language. DC 20 Insight to sense he's being deliberately evasive about what "signing the ledger" means.

## The Rakshasa: Mahavan the Collector

**Disguise**: Old human man, 70s, weathered and kind. Patched robes, bare feet, warm smile. His hands are gloved (hiding the backward-facing palms). DC 19 Perception to notice the gloves seem oddly shaped, as if the thumbs are on the wrong side.

**True Form**: Tiger-headed Rakshasa in silk robes, dripping with gold jewelry. If revealed, he's not angry — he's *amused*. "Clever girl. Does it change your answer?"

**Personality**: Genuinely enjoys music. Treats the wager as high art, not a scam. Will not cheat — his pride demands a fair victory. Condescending but not cruel. Applauds good playing even if he wins.

**Motivation**: He's collected 47 mortal artists' souls over three centuries. Each one plays for him in his palace on the Material Plane (he has a demiplane). Liz would be #48. He doesn't torture them — they play in his court. It's still horrifying, but he sees it as *patronage*.

**If the party attacks**: He sighs, says "How disappointing," and plane shifts away. The flute stays — but it's now just a mundane golden flute worth 200 gp. The magic was his, not the instrument's. He may return later to offer the wager again.

**Key Rakshasa traits to remember**:
- Immune to spells 6th level or below (party can't compel him)
- Limited Magic Immunity makes combat at level 5 essentially unwinnable
- Can read thoughts at will (he knows if the party is planning treachery)
- Can plane shift at will (he can't be trapped)

## Running the Contest

Choose **one** of these approaches based on your table's preferences:

---

### Option A: The Classic Duel (Fastest — 5 min)

Best of 3 contested **Performance checks**.
- Mahavan's Performance: **+12**
- Liz can use Bardic Inspiration on herself, any relevant spells or features
- Other PCs **cannot Help** — this is Liz's contest — but they can shout encouragement (flavor only) or use class features that don't require the Help action

**Round flow**: Mahavan plays → Liz plays → compare rolls. Best 2 of 3.

**Pros**: Fast, dramatic, dice-driven.
**Cons**: Swingy. Three lucky or unlucky rolls decide everything.

---

### Option B: The Skill Challenge (Best Balance — 15-20 min)

**3 successes before 3 failures** across up to 5 rounds. Each round tests a different musical virtue:

| Round | Theme | Check | DC | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Technical Mastery** | Performance | 17 | Pure skill — scales, arpeggios, precision |
| 2 | **Emotional Truth** | Persuasion | 15 | Play something *real* — RP what she draws on |
| 3 | **Improvisation** | Performance | 16 (disadvantage) | Mahavan plays something wild; she must answer |
| 4 | **The Audience** | Player's choice | 14 | If PCs are watching — win the crowd (any CHA skill) |
| 5 | **The Finale** | Performance | 18 | Final movement — but each prior success gives +1 |

**Party involvement**: Each PC can assist **once** during the challenge. They must narrate how (stomping rhythm, singing harmony, intimidating the old man's concentration). Grants advantage on that round's check.

**Cutting Words**: Liz can use Cutting Words to reduce Mahavan's "counter-performance" — effectively lowering a DC by the Bardic Inspiration die roll for one round.

**Pros**: Structured tension, multiple chances, party can contribute.
**Cons**: More bookkeeping.

---

### Option C: The Narrative Duel (Most Dramatic — 20-30 min)

Three **movements**, each with a theme Mahavan sets:

1. **"Play me your sorrow."** — Mahavan wants to hear loss. (Her widow backstory.)
2. **"Play me your defiance."** — He wants fire. The refusal to break.
3. **"Play me your joy."** — The hardest one. Joy after loss is what separates artists from craftsmen.

**Mechanics**: Base DC 16 Performance for each movement. Adjust ±2 based on RP quality:
- Player describes the music vividly, ties it to character history → DC 14
- Player gives a basic description → DC 16
- Player says "I play something sad" with no elaboration → DC 18

**Mahavan's music manifests minor illusions** during his turns — Liz sees flashes of her past, her dead spouse, her travels. These are his Read Thoughts ability at work. He's not being cruel; he's showing her he *understands*. It's intimate and violating at the same time.

**Pros**: Deep RP, ties directly to Liz's unexplored backstory, memorable scene.
**Cons**: Requires a player comfortable with spotlight RP. Check with Liz's player in advance.

---

### Option D: The Infernal Gambit (Hardest — 15-20 min)

Each of 3 rounds, Liz must make **two checks**:
1. **WIS save DC 15** — Mahavan's music carries enchantment magic (his Innate Spellcasting). Failure = disadvantage on the Performance check.
2. **Performance check** — contested vs. Mahavan's +12.

**Winning a round**: Beat his Performance roll (after any disadvantage).
**Majority wins** (2 of 3).

**Cutting Words**: Can reduce his Performance roll.
**Countercharm**: If she has it prepared, auto-succeed the WIS save for one round.

**Pros**: Highest mechanical tension, uses multiple abilities.
**Cons**: Stacks the deck against her. Two checks per round means more failure points.

---

## Foreshadowing & Clues

Drop these in the session *before* the encounter:

- A merchant on the road mentions hearing "the most beautiful flute music" coming from the hills at night, but found no one when he investigated
- A bird (raven or crow) has been following the party for a day — it's Mahavan's familiar-equivalent, scrying on them
- Liz specifically feels watched during a long rest. Nothing on Perception check — because Rakshasa mind-reading has no visible component
- An NPC in the last town mentions an old folk tale: "Never wager with a stranger on the road. Grandmother said the old ones walk the highways looking for pride."

## Win: The Golden Flute of Mahavan

Mahavan applauds — genuinely impressed. He stands, bows, and hands over the flute.

> "Forty-seven souls I've collected. You would have been the finest. Keep the flute, elfling — you've earned it. But know this: I will remember your music. And I will be listening."

He walks down the road and vanishes around the bend. If anyone follows, he's gone.

### The Golden Flute
*Wondrous Item (Instrument), Rare, Requires Attunement by a Bard*

- Functions as a +2 bardic spellcasting focus
- While attuned: **+2 to all Charisma-based ability checks** (Persuasion, Performance, Deception, Intimidation, and any other CHA checks)
- Does NOT apply to Charisma saving throws or spell attack rolls
- Made of an unknown golden metal, warm to the touch, impossibly light
- When played under moonlight, a faint smell of sandalwood and something ancient
- Cannot be destroyed by nonmagical means

**Hidden property (don't reveal immediately)**: Mahavan can hear the flute whenever it's played. He's not spying — he's listening to music. But he always knows where Liz is when she plays. This is flavor for future encounters, not a mechanical disadvantage.

### Additional Win Reward — Reputation
Word spreads. An elf who outplayed a fiend. Bards in taverns start telling the story (embellished wildly). Future Charisma checks with musically-inclined NPCs could get situational advantage.

### On Offering a Level-Up for Winning
Not recommended. The Golden Flute (+2 focus AND +2 to all CHA checks) is already one of the strongest items in the party. Adding a free level on top overloads the reward. If you want to give *something* extra, consider **Inspiration** (the 5e mechanic) or a free Feat at next level-up tied to the story (e.g., a homebrew "Devil's Rival" feat).

## Loss: Consequences (Choose One)

Mahavan wins. He doesn't gloat. He nods respectfully, produces the leather ledger, and holds out a quill.

> "You played beautifully. Truly. But beauty alone is not enough. Sign here, please. A wager is a wager."

If Liz refuses to sign after losing, Mahavan's demeanor shifts — not to anger, but to cold disappointment. "A wager made in good faith, broken in bad. That is *far* worse than losing, child." He could enforce the contract magically (the verbal agreement is binding in Infernal law), but the DM should decide if that's the route. Options below cover both voluntary and involuntary outcomes.

---

### Consequence 1: The Warlock Pact (Imposed)
Liz's next level must be taken as **Warlock (Fiend patron)**. Mahavan is her patron.

- Her Eldritch Blast manifests as a discordant musical chord
- Mahavan whispers advice in her dreams — always helpful, always *correct*, which is worse
- She didn't choose this; the pact was the price. Other PCs (especially [[Private/1. The Party/Players/Claire|Claire]]) would sense something changed
- **Offer an immediate level-up to 6 (Bard 5 / Warlock 1)**. This sweetens the loss and makes it feel like a narrative beat rather than a punishment. The player gains power she didn't want — classic Faustian bargain

**Best for**: A player who'd enjoy the RP of unwanted power. Talk to Liz's player first.

### Consequence 2: Soul Mark (Ticking Clock)
Her soul is claimed but not collected. A golden brand appears on her left palm — visible, warm, faintly humming.

- **30 days** to void the contract (find a loophole, challenge a rematch, get divine intervention)
- Until resolved: **disadvantage on death saving throws** — her soul is being pulled toward Mahavan's demiplane
- Creates a **side quest arc**: researching Infernal contract law, finding a cleric or celestial patron strong enough to break the bond, or training for a rematch
- Mahavan is *patient*. He's not chasing her. The clock is the pressure.

**Best for**: Creating urgency and a multi-session story arc without forcing a class change.

### Consequence 3: The Standing Offer (Psychological)
Mahavan takes nothing. Not yet.

> "I own your soul now, child. But I am patient — I have centuries. When you are ready to accept what that means, play three descending notes on any instrument. I will hear. I will come. And I will give you power you cannot imagine."

- No mechanical penalty. The punishment is *knowing*.
- The warlock pact is available whenever she chooses — she just has to play three notes
- Other PCs don't notice anything changed. Only Liz knows.
- Mahavan occasionally sends dreams — not nightmares. Visions of his court, the music, the beauty of it. "See? It's not so terrible."

**Best for**: A slow burn. The player decides if and when to engage. Maximum player agency.

### Consequence 4: Infernal Debt (One Favor)
No soul taken. Instead, Mahavan owns **one favor** — to be called in at the worst possible moment.

- Mahavan will appear at a dramatically devastating time and ask Liz to do something
- Could be "play at my banquet" (pulling her away from the party for a session), "deliver this letter" (to someone dangerous), or "convince your friends to spare this person" (an NPC the party wants dead who is Mahavan's associate)
- **The favor cannot be refused** — the contract compels compliance
- The DM gets a narrative bomb to deploy whenever it's perfect. Nothing changes mechanically until then.

**Best for**: DMs who want a tool in their back pocket without immediate consequences.

### Consequence 5: Creeping Corruption (Gradual)
Warlock magic starts manifesting *involuntarily*, whether Liz takes a warlock level or not:

- Week 1: She sees perfectly in darkness. She didn't ask for it.
- Week 2: When startled, a burst of eldritch energy fires from her hand (`dice: 1d10` force damage to the nearest object)
- Week 3: She understands Infernal. She's never studied it.
- Week 4: An NPC remarks that her music "sounds different now — beautiful, but... cold."

The powers are there, growing. She can take Warlock levels to *control* them, or resist and deal with the chaos. Claire would notice the fiendish taint with Divine Sense.

**Best for**: A "body horror" arc. The player's agency is in how they *respond*, not whether it happens.

### On the Immediate Level-Up (For Losing)
**Recommended for Consequence 1 (Warlock Pact)**: Yes. Bard 5 / Warlock 1 immediately. The player gets *something* out of losing, and "power at a cost" is the whole point. Without the level-up, it just feels like the DM took her choice away.

**Not needed for other consequences**: 2-5 don't force a class change, so there's no mechanical loss to compensate for.

### Mahavan the Collector (Rakshasa)

**Skills:** Deception +10, Insight +8, Performance +12
**Damage Vulnerabilities:** Piercing from magic weapons wielded by good creatures
**Damage Immunities:** Bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing from nonmagical attacks
**Senses:** Darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 13
**Languages:** Common, Infernal, Elvish, Sylvan
**CR:** 13 (10,000 XP)

**Traits:**

**Limited Magic Immunity.** Mahavan can't be affected or detected by spells of 6th level or lower unless he wishes to be. He has advantage on saving throws against all other spells and magical effects.

**Innate Spellcasting.** Mahavan's innate spellcasting ability is Charisma (spell save DC 18, +10 to hit with spell attacks). He can innately cast the following spells, requiring no material components:
- At will: *detect thoughts, disguise self, mage hand, minor illusion*
- 3/day each: *charm person, detect magic, invisibility, major image, suggestion*
- 1/day each: *dominate person, fly, plane shift, true seeing*

**Additional (Custom):**
- **Master Musician.** +12 to Performance checks. His music can produce the effects of *minor illusion* without using a spell slot.
- **Soul Ledger.** Mahavan carries a magical ledger. A creature that willingly signs it is bound by an Infernal contract. Breaking the contract requires *wish*, divine intervention, or defeating Mahavan on his home plane.

**Actions:**

**Multiattack.** Mahavan makes two claw attacks.

**Claw.** Melee Weapon Attack: +7 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. Hit: 9 (2d6 + 2) slashing damage, and the target is cursed if it is not a Construct or Undead. The curse takes effect whenever the target takes a short or long rest, filling the target's thoughts with horrible images and dreams. The cursed target gains no benefit from finishing a short or long rest. The curse lasts until it is lifted by a *remove curse* spell or similar magic.

**Plane Shift.** Mahavan can plane shift to the Material Plane or his demiplane as an action.

## Character Spotlight

### [[Private/1. The Party/Players/Liz|Liz]]
**This is her encounter.** Everything revolves around her choice.

**Key stats:**
- Performance: +9 (proficiency + CHA 17)
- Charisma: 17 (+3)
- Bardic Inspiration: d8 (can use on herself per College of Lore)
- Cutting Words: reaction to subtract Bardic Inspiration die from enemy's roll
- Relevant spells: Check her spell list for anything that boosts Performance or imposes disadvantage

**The RP opportunity:** Mahavan's music during the contest conjures illusions from Liz's past — her dead spouse, her elven homeland, her travels. This is the first time her **widow backstory** gets explored at the table. Let the player run with it.

### Other PCs — What Can They Do?

The contest is Liz's, but the party shouldn't just sit and watch:

- **Claire** (Cleric): Can use Divine Sense to detect Mahavan as a fiend. This is the big reveal moment — does she tell Liz before or during the contest? Does it change anything?
- **Randi** (Divination Wizard): Portent dice. If Randi has a high Portent roll saved, she could gift it to Liz for a Performance check. Narratively: "I foresaw this moment."
- **JP** (Monk): Could attempt to steal the ledger (Sleight of Hand vs. Mahavan's Perception +3). If successful, no contract can be signed regardless of outcome.
- **Julie** (Fighter): Could Intimidate Mahavan (good luck — he's +5 CHA). Or spot the backward hands (Investigation/Perception DC 19).
- **Justin** (Ranger): Favored Enemy / Primeval Awareness could detect fiend presence depending on his build. Good for confirming Claire's suspicion.

## DM Decision Points

Before running this, decide:

- [ ] Which **contest mechanic** (A/B/C/D)?
- [ ] Which **loss consequence** (1-5)?
- [ ] Does Liz get an **immediate level-up** if she loses and takes warlock?
- [ ] Does Claire's **Divine Sense** auto-detect Mahavan, or does she need to actively use it?
- [ ] If the party **identifies him as a Rakshasa** before the contest, does Mahavan drop the disguise and offer the wager openly?
- [ ] Can the party **talk Liz out of it**? (Recommendation: let Liz decide. Player agency.)
- [ ] If Liz **refuses the wager entirely**, does Mahavan leave peacefully or does he try to entice her?
