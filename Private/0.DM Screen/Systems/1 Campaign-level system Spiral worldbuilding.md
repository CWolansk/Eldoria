

This is the best “bottom-up” campaign-building method.

Instead of building the gods, continents, empires, calendar, trade routes, and cosmology first, you start with what matters to the characters **now**, then spiral outward.

Sly Flourish’s **spiral campaign development** starts with the campaign’s theme, then focuses on the characters’ starting area: where they begin, what makes that place unique, which NPCs matter, what villains are active nearby, and what nearby adventure locations exist.

Use this campaign checklist:

## Campaign sheet

Answer these once at the start, then revise between arcs.

1. **Campaign promise:** What is this campaign about in one sentence?  
    Example: “A frontier town is slowly being swallowed by an ancient fey forest.”
2. **Three to six truths:** What makes this world different?  
    Example: “The gods are silent,” “iron burns fey creatures,” “the old empire never truly fell.”
3. **Starting place:** One town, outpost, ship, guildhall, city district, or caravan.
4. **Three local powers:** Factions, rulers, temples, gangs, noble houses, cults, guilds.
5. **Three nearby adventure sites:** Ruin, cave, haunted manor, political court, forest shrine, war camp.
6. **Three important NPCs:** Give each one a desire, fear, and something they can offer.
7. **Three active threats:** These become your fronts, explained below.
8. **Two horizons:** What can the PCs see right now, and what are three things just beyond that? Sly Flourish’s “two horizons” idea is to know enough about the next layer of possible adventure that you can drop hooks and foreshadowing without preparing the whole world.

This keeps you from overbuilding. You only need the world that can touch the PCs soon.
