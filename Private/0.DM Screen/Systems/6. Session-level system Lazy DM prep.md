# 

This is probably the most useful repeatable prep checklist for every session.

Sly Flourish’s **Lazy DM** method has eight steps:

1. Review the characters
2. Create a strong start
3. Outline potential scenes
4. Define secrets and clues
5. Develop fantastic locations
6. Outline important NPCs
7. Choose relevant monsters
8. Select magic item rewards

Those steps are meant to focus prep on material that helps you improvise, not on writing a fixed story.

## My version of the session worksheet

Before each session, fill this out:

### 1. Characters

For each PC:

- What do they want?
- What unresolved hook touches them?
- What can spotlight them this session?

### 2. Strong start

Start with something happening.

Examples:

- The bridge collapses.
- The duke’s messenger arrives wounded.
- The prisoner says, “I lied.”
- The tavern door opens and everyone goes silent.
- The villain offers a deal.

### 3. Likely scenes

Write three to five possible scenes, not mandatory scenes.

Example:

- Questioning the captured cultist
- Traveling through the drowned road
- Meeting the witch of the mill
- Ambush by drowned knights
- Discovering the sunken chapel

### 4. Secrets and clues

Write ten pieces of information the players might discover.

Do **not** attach each clue to only one location. Let them surface wherever it makes sense.

Example:

- The drowned knights still obey the last royal command.
- The witch was once the queen’s physician.
- The chapel bell rings only when someone lies inside it.
- The cult does not worship the dead god; it fears him.

### 5. Locations

Write three evocative locations.

- The chapel with pews floating in black water
- The mill whose wheel turns without wind
- The graveyard where every name has been scratched away

### 6. NPCs

For each NPC:

- Name
- Want
- Fear
- Secret
- Voice/mannerism

### 7. Obstacles

Combat, traps, social resistance, travel hazards, puzzles, moral dilemmas.

### 8. Rewards

Treasure, information, allies, titles, favors, maps, emotional payoff.

### 9. End-of-session choice

End by asking:

> “What do you plan to do next?”

That one habit makes your next prep much easier.
