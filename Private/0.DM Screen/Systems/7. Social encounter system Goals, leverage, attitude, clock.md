# 

Social encounters become much easier if you stop thinking of them as “roleplay until someone rolls Persuasion.”

Use this instead.

## NPC social card

For every important NPC, prep:

**Name:** Captain Mira Voss  
**Wants:** Keep the city safe  
**Fears:** Being blamed for another massacre  
**Will offer:** Guards, records, access to prisoners  
**Will not do:** Openly defy the duke without proof  
**Secret:** Her brother joined the cult  
**Starting attitude:** Indifferent  
**Leverage:** Evidence, public pressure, appeal to duty, promise of protection  
**Pressure point:** Harm to civilians  
**Tell:** Taps her sword hilt when lying

D&D’s 2024 free rules describe NPC attitudes as **Friendly, Indifferent, or Hostile**, and say social interaction proceeds through roleplaying and ability checks; the DM uses the NPC’s personality and the characters’ actions to determine how the NPC reacts.

## Running the social scene

Use this procedure:

1. **What do the PCs want from the NPC?**  
    Be specific. “Help us” is vague. “Give us access to the prison records” is clear.
2. **Is the NPC willing, unwilling, or persuadable?**  
    If willing, no roll. If absolutely opposed, they need new leverage. If persuadable, continue.
3. **Set starting attitude:** Friendly, indifferent, hostile.
4. **Roleplay to find leverage:** Let the players discover wants, fears, ideals, secrets, obligations, guilt, greed, pride, loyalty.
5. **Let arguments change the situation:** Good leverage improves attitude or lowers DC. Bad tactics worsen attitude or create consequences.
6. **Roll only when the outcome is uncertain.**
7. **Use clocks for extended scenes.**

Example:

**Convince the council:** 0/6  
**Council loses patience:** 0/4  
**Assassin reaches the chamber:** 0/6

Each strong argument, useful fact, bribe, emotional appeal, or successful check fills the “convince” clock. Each insult, failed check, delay, or enemy interference fills the danger clock.

This makes social play feel like a real encounter without turning it into fake combat.
