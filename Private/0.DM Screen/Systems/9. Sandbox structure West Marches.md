# 

A **West Marches** campaign is useful if you want player-driven exploration. The classic structure is an open-table sandbox where players choose where they want to go, organize the party, and often tell the GM the destination before the session. A recent summary describes it as a style introduced through Ben Robbins’s 2007 blog posts, centered on a sandbox setting, player self-organization, and self-contained expeditions.

Use West Marches thinking even if you do not run a true open table.

The useful principles are:

- A safe home base.
- A dangerous surrounding map.
- Rumors pointing to locations.
- Players choose the next expedition.
- The world changes based on what they ignore or disturb.
- Information matters: maps, rumors, journals, scars, survivor stories.

This is great for exploration campaigns because prep becomes location-based instead of plot-based.
