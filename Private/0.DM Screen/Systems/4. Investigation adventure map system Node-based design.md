Use **node-based design** when the adventure involves investigation, mystery, politics, travel between locations, or multiple leads.

A **node** is a place, person, event, or situation. Each node contains clues pointing to other nodes. The Alexandrian’s node-based advice emphasizes that the adventure keeps working as long as the PCs have somewhere else to investigate; a key related idea is that PCs should have access to enough clues so the scenario does not break if they miss or misread one.

## Simple node map

Opening node: Murdered knight in alley  
Points to:

- The alchemist who made the poison
- The noble house that owned the dagger
- The sewer shrine where the killer fled

Each of those nodes points to at least two others.

You can sketch this as bubbles and arrows. This is much better than prepping a linear mystery.

Use node design when you ask:

> “How do I make sure the players can move through the adventure without railroading them?”
