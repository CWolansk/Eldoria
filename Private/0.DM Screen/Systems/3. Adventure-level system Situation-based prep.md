# 3. Adventure-level system: Situation-based prep

For an adventure, do not write:

> “The players go to the inn, then meet the spy, then follow him to the warehouse, then fight the cultists.”

Write:

> “A spy has proof that the duke is working with the cult. The cult wants to kill the spy. The duke wants the evidence destroyed. The spy wants safe passage. The warehouse, inn, sewer route, and chapel are all relevant places.”

That is a situation.

## Adventure prep checklist

Use this whenever you build an adventure.

1. **Core conflict:** What unstable situation exists?  
    Example: “The baron’s heir has been kidnapped, but the kidnapper claims the heir is a monster.”
2. **Opposing sides:** Who wants incompatible things?
3. **Villain/faction plan:** What happens if PCs do nothing?
4. **Adventure locations:** Three to seven places where meaningful things can happen.
5. **NPCs:** Give each one a goal, fear, secret, and leverage.
6. **Clues:** What information lets the PCs understand the situation?
7. **Three routes forward:** For any necessary conclusion, include at least three ways to learn it or bypass it. This is the Alexandrian’s Three Clue Rule applied practically.
8. **Consequences:** What changes if the PCs succeed, fail, delay, or choose a strange third option?

This is the most important adventure-building system, in my opinion.
