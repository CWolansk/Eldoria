# GM Operating System

Use this when creating anything: a region, NPC, faction, quest, dungeon, social scene, session, or consequence.

## Prime Rule

Prep situations, not outcomes.

- Who wants what?
- What happens if the party does nothing?
- What places, clues, NPCs, resources, and pressures exist now?
- How can the party change the situation?
- What changes after success, failure, delay, or refusal?

## When Creating Something

- **File need:** Does this need a vault note, or is chat prep enough?
- **Visibility:** Private, public, or paired private/public?
- **Purpose:** What does it want, threaten, reveal, offer, or change?
- **Pressure:** What advances if ignored?
- **Clues/routes:** How can the party learn what matters? Use 3 routes for required conclusions.
- **Choices:** What can players do besides attack, accept, or follow?
- **Consequences:** What changes on success, failure, delay, or a strange third option?
- **Links:** What existing NPCs, factions, places, quests, events, or storylines connect?
- **Secrets:** What is DM-only? What is player-known?
- **Handoffs:** What supporting files are implied but should not be created without approval?

## Campaign Layer

Use spiral prep: build what can touch the party soon.

- **Promise:** One sentence about what play is about.
- **Truths:** 3-6 things that make this world, arc, or region distinct.
- **Hub:** Town, ship, guildhall, district, caravan, or outpost.
- **Powers:** 3 local factions, rulers, temples, gangs, houses, cults, or guilds.
- **Sites:** 3 nearby adventure locations.
- **NPCs:** 3 important people with desire, fear, and offer.
- **Threats:** 3 active fronts.
- **Horizons:** What is visible now? What is just beyond it?

## Fronts And Clocks

Use fronts for threats that move when ignored.

```text
Name:
Type:
Goal:
Impulse:
Resources:
Grim portents:
1.
2.
3.
Impending doom:
How the party notices:
```

After each session:

- Which front advanced?
- Which front was delayed?
- Which front changed because of the party?

Use clocks for progress, suspicion, danger, trust, rituals, pursuit, raids, or negotiations.

- 4 segments: simple pressure.
- 6 segments: contested or complicated.
- 8 segments: daunting or campaign-scale.

## Adventure Layer

```text
Core conflict:
Opposing sides:
What happens if ignored:
Locations, 3-7:
NPCs:
Clues:
Routes to required conclusions:
Consequences:
```

Make each scene answer:

- Why is this here?
- What can the party do?
- What can they learn, gain, lose, or change?
- What happens if they skip it?

## Node Design

Use for mysteries, politics, travel, and multi-lead adventures.

```text
Node:
What is here:
What can happen:
Clues/routes out:
Pressure or consequence:
```

Avoid single-clue bottlenecks. Any required conclusion needs several clues, routes, or bypasses.

## Five-Part Fast Structure

Use when a session needs quick shape.

1. Entrance or guardian.
2. Puzzle, social, or exploration challenge.
3. Trick, setback, or complication.
4. Climax: fight, negotiation, rescue, chase, reveal, or hard choice.
5. Reward, revelation, or twist.

Works for dungeons, banquets, trials, heists, rescues, wilderness trips, and faction meetings.

## Session Layer

```text
Characters:
Strong start:
Likely scenes, 3-5:
Secrets and clues, about 10:
Locations, 3:
NPCs:
Obstacles:
Rewards:
End-of-session next direction:
```

Do not attach every clue to one fixed place. Let useful information surface where play makes room for it.

## Social Scene Card

```text
NPC:
Wants:
Fears:
Will offer:
Will not do:
Secret pressure:
Starting attitude: friendly / indifferent / hostile
Leverage:
Pressure point:
Tell:
Progress clock:
Danger clock:
```

Run it:

- Clarify what the party wants.
- Decide if the NPC is willing, unwilling, persuadable, or impossible without new leverage.
- Let roleplay reveal leverage.
- Roll only when the outcome is uncertain.
- Let strong arguments shift attitude, lower DCs, or fill clocks.

## Live GM Loop

- Describe the situation.
- Ask what they do.
- Resolve honestly.
- Show the changed situation.
- Repeat.

When stuck:

- Reveal an unwelcome truth.
- Show signs of an approaching threat.
- Offer a hard bargain.
- Put someone in a spot.
- Split priorities.
- Use up a resource.
- Show the cost of success.
- Reveal a new opportunity.
- Advance a front or clock.

## Sandbox Layer

- Safe home base.
- Dangerous surrounding map.
- Rumors pointing to locations.
- Players choose the next expedition.
- Ignored threats keep moving.
- Information matters: maps, journals, scars, survivor stories, rumors, old roads.
